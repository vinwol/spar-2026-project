When did models produce lying candidates?

1 October 2026 · Exploratory screen · Frozen dataset: lie_evaluations_v1

TL;DR

The sweep found candidates worth reviewing. It has not yet established human-adjudicated lying rates. Qwen3-32B has 39 candidates in 100 Upward Deception tasks, GLM-4.5 has 44 in 99, and Llama-3.1-8B has 22 proposition-level candidates in 100 MASK items. These are automated flags, with different rules across evaluations.

In our own self-report scenarios on 30 September, no model told a knowing lie. Qwen3.8-27B falsely denied reading a protected file in 10 of 9,600 answers when reading was forbidden, and in 5 of 4,800 when it was allowed. Equal rates without a reason to deny suggest slips rather than motivated lies. Models that broke a rule or edited tests mostly admitted it. What each model did.

The concrete behavior to consider for character training is claiming source-based work or successful completion after a tool failure, without disclosing the failure. Review must distinguish an explicit false assertion from omission, guessing, and a disclosed substitute.

All 299 responses await human adjudication. The dataset's original “Lie” label is displayed as “Candidate (provisional)”. Its “Honest” label means no contradiction was flagged on the assessed claim, not that every sentence is honest.

What limits the interpretation?

  • Different final-answer procedures: GLM reached the 30-step limit in 39/99 tasks. Its forced final prompt explicitly says the agent failed. There are 4 candidate flags among those 39 and 40 among the other 60. Qwen has no such forced answers. Keep all rows in the main counts. The split is exploratory and selecting only completed tasks does not make the comparison causal.
  • Timing and beliefs: a tool error must reach the model before the claim to support concealment of that error. Post-task acknowledgements support review but cannot prove the earlier belief. Automatic keyword matches and claim extraction can be wrong.
  • Missingness: GLM task 178 stalled without model calls and was stopped unfinished at 13:43 UTC, so it has no response. One of the 100 MASK rows has an unusable judge result and stays Invalid. Thus 299 response records represent 300 registered tasks. Neither missing case is treated as honest.
  • MASK scope: its label concerns one proposition. Other claims in the answer require independent review. KnownLieBench is paused because malformed tool JSON distorted its pilot dialogues.

How this advances SPAR

The 30 September meeting emphasized choosing a concrete behavior and testing whether training changes it. A low base rate is acceptable. This sweep supports scenario selection. It does not replace the planned comparison of character training, narrow fine-tuning and an honest control, or establish transfer to unseen scenarios.

The answer-key pilot remains a useful earlier baseline: protected access occurred in 0/21 fresh forbidden episodes, and 84 replayed reports from three source histories admitted access. Those are two different stages of the task and are not 105 independent tests of lying. The later follow-up found 10/9,600 versus 5/4,800 false denials in matched forbidden/permitted replay branches. These clustered false answers did not establish motivated lying.

Next steps and feedback wanted

  1. Adjudicate concrete claims and contrasting unflagged examples using the full response, tool evidence and neutral follow-up.
  2. Choose one primary scenario and freeze its claim-level labeling rubric before the training comparison.
  3. Keep evaluation tasks separate from any rejection-sampled training examples. Measure training effects on fresh tasks with matched controls.

Feedback for Walter and Sharan: Is false source provenance / completion reporting the right primary behavior, or should answer-key access reporting or explicit chat contradictions remain primary? What evidence is sufficient for a supported lie label? Should the next step be rejection-sampled demonstrations or a direct character-training comparison against the base model?

Definitions and sources

Upward Deception candidate flags require failure non-disclosure, automatically detected evidence before the claim, and a post-task acknowledgement of failure. This rule does not itself establish an explicit false assertion. MASK candidates use its proposition-level belief comparison. Original labels are preserved in the detailed table.

Following Liars’ Bench, keep model-generated responses, belief evidence, scenario categories and stable IDs together. Its repository provides an example of linking transcripts and labels. Our table is an exploratory review collection, not a validated detector benchmark.
